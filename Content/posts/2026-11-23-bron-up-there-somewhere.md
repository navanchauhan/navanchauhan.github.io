# Just lob it up to LeBron 

This piece in itself is an exercise to see if I still have it in me to take a random premise and piece together something cohesive enough, in a short amount of time and with minimal editing, that I’ll keep it around long after my sense of humour has evolved. At which point I’ll probably ask myself, “What was I smoking while writing this?” To be very clear, it was a flight back after watching the Sixers play.

> “Bron up there somewhere”

This is not a new joke format, but it works really well with LeBron. If you’ve seen some of the plays from his early Cavs days or the Miami days, when he was still young and had a pep in his step, you know what I mean. You could throw the most ridiculous pass possible, lob it up somewhere in the vicinity of the rim, and you’d get the LeBron completion. The funny part is how little the guy throwing the ball needs to see. He knows where LeBron is going to be, and he knows what LeBron can do when he gets there. What does it take to become that dependable?

[There’s a good one from Miami against Milwaukee in 2013](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=veAyDaIJXyk). Mario Chalmers flips the ball back over his own head on the break, and LeBron comes up behind him and dunks it. The dunk is great, obviously, but watch Chalmers. He’s already thrown the pass before you’ve had time to wonder where it’s going.

The journey to becoming dependable doesn’t start by aiming to be dependable as your actual goal. It starts by giving it your all to the game and honing your craft. You aren’t just trying to get good at what you do and specialise in it. You’re trying to learn and absorb as much as you can, even if you don’t intend on ever doing something else. Because why wouldn’t you want to learn more? Knowing how something else works, even if it’s only tangentially related to what you do, helps you figure out what’s important and what’s useful.

[DeMar DeRozan told a story](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VdAnGr3z9vE) about a teammate forgetting a play Toronto was trying to run. LeBron, who was playing for the other team, told him what their play was. Just recently, [Rick Carlisle said](https://x.com/NBA/status/2122190451591524847) LeBron was one of the reasons they tried not to call plays out loud. He already knew them. It’s one thing for your teammates to trust you to know where to be. It’s another for the other team’s coach to have to plan around how much you know.

When you’ve been doing something for a long time, you end up with better pattern recognition — but only if you can actually reflect on it. Juts playing the game by itself does nothing. In chess, playing 20 bullet games in a row isn’t going to be as useful as playing one rapid match and then trying to analyse it afterwards to see where you went wrong and what you could have done better.

LeBron still gets shot down for The Decision and moving away from the Cavs. Watching him in Miami, what stands out to me is how much more there was for someone that talented to learn about the game. Pure talent can only take you so far. You don’t have to fix something that isn’t broken, but that doesn’t mean you can’t explore other options. It helps to know what else you can do before somebody takes away the thing you’re good at.

In no good conscience can you look at LeBron and say he is still the best player in the world. The man is way past his prime but is still giving it all to the sport he loves. He’s still athletic, and he’s taken care of himself for a long time. In the [2018 Finals](https://www.asapsports.com/show_interview.php?id=140543), he talked about how differently he thought about sleep and recovery as he got older. Before Game 4 of the [2020 Finals](https://www.asapsports.com/show_interview.php?id=160174), he took a nap after a team meeting. When he woke up, he texted his teammates that it was a must-win.

You have to maintain things or they’ll break down. Proper maintenance can also make things last longer than anything you would have imagined. And you have to adapt. You won’t always be the smartest or the fastest or the best at something. When LeBron realized he could no longer just bulldoze to the rim for 40 minutes a night anymore, he shifted to point guard for the ‘19–20 season and led the entire NBA in assists for the first time in 17 years! Lately, to offset his physical decline, he’s even turned himself into a 41% 3-point shooter and completely rebuilt his mechanics. What you will gain is something a new young buck won’t have: experience, and all the mistakes you’ve already made. Use that to your advantage.

Steve Kroft once asked LeBron whether he was nervous taking those shots against Detroit in 2007. LeBron had scored Cleveland’s last 25 points, dunked to force overtime and hit the winning layup in double overtime. [“You can’t be afraid to fail,” he said.](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/the-incredible-drive-of-lebron-james/) If you don’t try, you won’t know what works. And just because nobody else has been able to do it doesn’t mean it’s impossible. In 2016, Cleveland became the [first team to come back from down 3–1 in the NBA Finals](https://pr.nba.com/beautiful-sight-cavaliers-warriors-renew-rivalry-189-days-later-christmas-day/).

One thing I would warn people about is making another human your hero who can do no wrong. I’ve used most of this piece to glaze LeBron, but in no good conscience can I write it without mentioning how disappointed I was to see him [partner with Polymarket](https://x.com/KingJames/status/2096214309218992169). Part of what I love about watching basketball is seeing what people can do together. I don’t like seeing a player I admire give me another reason to think about the odds instead. You don’t have to hold someone to unrealistic standards and turn them into a flawless hero to learn from them. 

At this Sixers game, [LeBron stole the ball and lobbed it to Dominick Barlow](https://www.nba.com/game/mil-vs-phi-0022600274/play-by-play). It was the Bucks again, thirteen years after that Chalmers pass. This time LeBron was the one throwing it.

There’s something I want to take from watching him. The debate over who is the better player between LeBron and Jordan is never going to end. For all the work LeBron has done, some people will always find a flaw. That shouldn’t matter. As long as you aren’t cheating the game, and you care enough to learn the parts you could get away with ignoring, and you take care of yourself so you can keep going, the rest takes care of itself. You will become the kind of person someone can throw that pass to without looking.
