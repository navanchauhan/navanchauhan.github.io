## Atrophy, Training to Failure, and the Wager

<!--

- Unused muscles atrophy; comfort-zone inertia locks you in ("why fix what ain't broke?" — because you're a person, not a static service).
- Restaurant analogy + Gittins index: At a new restaurant I explore until I find one dish I love, then order it forever — so the only way to explore again is switching restaurants. (Optional sidenote: Gittins index is the formal math for optimal explore/exploit!)
- Pascal's Wager payoff table: Fear of failure feels rational until you realize *not trying* isn't a neutral 0 — you forfeit the upside AND atrophy.

| | It works out | It doesn't work out |
|---|---|---|
| **You try** | You win | Failed rep — you build capacity and learn where your actual limit is |
| **You don't try** | Impossible (already forfeited) | Guaranteed loss + atrophy (trying next time gets harder) |

You have probably already heard either me, or countless other people on the internet say by now to “be more ambitious.” But, is this another herd mentality take on the internet, or does it actually have some merit?
I am one of the laziest people I know. I don’t really want much in life. I would be so much happier just rotting on the couch and watching some TV. Okay, I am joking about that (I think). I am lazy, and I don’t really wake up wanting anything, but looking back I have almost always been up to some shenanigans. Just when it seems like I am running out of ideas, something new pops up. Everything always works out in the end somehow.
Maybe being more ambitious means breaking the cyclic nature of life and stepping out of your comfort zone. Trying something new. Trying something different. Trying something you were always scared of doing because you didn’t want to fail at something. You don’t have to shoot for the moon, you just need to ask for / do something that you didn’t before, or didn’t think was possible before.
I remember this advice in chess, when the opponent plays a move, you should look at the lines that were not possible before.
So, trick 1: “re-evaluate things that you thought weren’t possible before. Are any of them possible now?”
I used to have a whole backlog of silly ideas that I couldn’t even attempt before. Now, I can spin up an agent on my computer from my phone by asking Siri to text this iMessage agent completely hands-free while driving, and having initial results ready before I even get back home. How cool and cyberpunk is that?
When you are working out at the gym (I say as my lazy ass hasn’t gone in weeks), you try to train until failure. That is because only when you destroy your muscles do they come back stronger. This also teaches you to embrace failure. Without failing, you can’t come back stronger. Because if you don’t fail, then that means you aren’t pushing yourself enough and haven’t found your limits yet.
Trick 2 is to ask yourself: “When was the last time you actually failed at something, what is something you definitely know you would theoretically not be able to do, and you should go ahead and try it”
Also, if you don’t use these strong muscles for a long time, they begin to atrophy. Similarly, if you don’t keep on trying new things, it becomes harder to try new things. The inertia of the comfort zone is enough to lock you in. Why fix something that ain’t broke? But you aren’t a system that doesn’t need to be touched, you are a human being who needs to experience new things to realise what you are missing.
If I go to a new restaurant, I will keep trying new things until I find something I like. And, once I like something, I will almost always get the same thing. So, the only way for me to try new things then becomes changing the restaurant. Thankfully, I am ambivalent about trying new places.
