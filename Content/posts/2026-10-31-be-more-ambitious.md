## Atrophy, Training to Failure, and the Wager

<!--
SECTION 2: Atrophy + Gym Failure + Pascal's Wager
- Unused muscles atrophy. Skipping new things works the same way: every time you don't try something new, it gets easier to keep not trying until caution feels like your personality.
- Why be afraid of failure? In the gym, you literally train *until* failure — you can't build new muscle without tiring out the old ones. Why expect life to be any different? (Also check your unfinished note: "Being ambitious means to think ...")
- Lay out the Pascal's Wager payoff matrix: fear of failure feels rational, until you realize *not trying* isn't a neutral 0 — you automatically forfeit the upside AND atrophy. Row 1 strictly beats Row 2 in both columns.
-->

| | It works out | It doesn't work out |
|---|---|---|
| **You try** | You win | Failed rep — you build capacity and learn where your actual limit is |
| **You don't try** | Impossible (already forfeited) | Guaranteed loss + atrophy (trying next time gets harder) |



## "If Only I Had X" (And What Happens When Programming Is Solved)
